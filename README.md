# Crop Scouting

Crop Scouting plans a local tool that ranks corn-like crop regions for human inspection after unusual vegetation changes. It explains the imagery, seasonal baseline, cloud coverage and uncertainty behind each ranking. It does not diagnose disease, identify the cause of stress, recommend treatment or predict yield.

The first functional public release is planned for month 7 after activation: public-data ingestion, a transparent temporal baseline, a ranked map, trend plots and a scouting export. Automatically generated anomaly fixtures validate sensitivity and failure behavior. Public imagery establishes pipeline behavior; it does not supply agronomic truth. Crop-mask regions are clearly distinguished from farm ownership or verified field boundaries.

One portfolio project runs at a time, at 16 hours weekly. The budget is 640 work hours plus 160 reserve hours, completing within 50 active weeks without drones, farm partners, paid labeling or customer records.
